Group / Family Applications — June 2026 performance report

Observation window: 8–28 June 2026. Population: 1,200 unique groups and 5,453 lifecycle events in atlys.group_application_events.

Executive summary

  • Overall completion: 688 of 1,200 groups submitted, for a 57.3% group_started → group_submitted completion rate.
  • Group size is the strongest observed cut: completion fell from 69.5% for groups of two to 31.1% for groups of six.
  • Removal churn is concentrated in large groups: only 0.4% of two-person groups had a removal versus 17.8% of six-person groups.
  • Documents are incomplete for roughly one in five added travellers: docs_complete was true on 2,795 of 3,495 traveller_added events, or 80.0%.
  • Large groups are the priority: their low completion coincides with higher removal activity and lower document completeness, but the data does not prove that documents are the sole cause of abandonment.

There were 3,495 additions and 70 removals overall, a 2.0% removal-to-add ratio. Churn is not broad-based: 69 groups had at least one removal, and the concentration in groups of five and six suggests that large-group management is harder. This is consistent with coordination friction, but the available events do not identify whether a removal reflects correction, a traveller dropping out, or normal editing.

Document readiness declines only 2.7–3.3 percentage points between groups of two and groups of five or six, whereas submission completion declines 31.0–38.4 points. This makes documents a plausible contributor, but not a sufficient explanation for the large-group fall-off. A useful diagnostic is that 32.1% of six-person groups had every added traveller marked complete, compared with 65.2% of submitted two-person groups; however, this is partly a mechanical consequence of requiring more travellers to be complete. The feature should expose per-traveller outstanding-document status and measure the time from add to completion in a follow-up instrumentation revision.

Recommendations

  1. Prioritize groups of five and six. Add a clear progress summary showing completed and outstanding documents for every traveller, with a single resume link for incomplete co-travellers.
  2. Investigate removal points. Instrument the reason for traveller_removed and whether the removal occurs before or after document completion. The current table can measure churn volume but not intent.
  3. Separate group coordination from document friction. Add lifecycle events for invitation sent/opened, co-traveller document-requested, document-started, and document-completed timestamps.
  4. Run destination-by-size cuts before intervention rollout. Thailand, US, and UAE have material volume but lower completion than Indonesia, GB, and France; control for group-size mix before labelling destinations as problematic.
  5. Improve attribution fields. Add citizenship, acquisition/channel, and explicit co-traveller metadata so the PM questions can be answered by traveller segment and source.

Method and data-quality notes

The table is append-only MergeTree data ordered by group and event time. It contains 5,453 rows, 5,453 unique event IDs, and no records with ingest_version > 1 in this extract, so no observed deduplication adjustment was required. Group-level metrics were reconstructed by group_id; group size and destination were taken from group_started. Completion means any matching group_submitted, not necessarily a timestamp-ordered sequence. The dataset covers only 21 calendar days, and later lifecycle events do not consistently repeat cohort fields, so conclusions should be treated as descriptive rather than causal.
