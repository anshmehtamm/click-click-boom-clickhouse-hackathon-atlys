Where are we losing conversions?

Bottom line: the dominant loss is the application-to-document step, not a particular destination or device. The second major loss is pay-now to purchase.

The document-upload loss represents approximately 134,000 applications, making it the highest-priority conversion problem by volume. The payment loss represents approximately 7,685 pay-now users who did not complete purchase.

Android is the clearest device-level weakness: its start-to-purchase rate is 4.13% versus 5.03% on iOS, and its document threshold-crossing rate is 16.22% versus 8.70% on iOS. The Android issue is therefore visible both at document capture and at payment completion. Web/Mac has the weakest payment step, with a 56.41% pay-to-purchase drop, but its smaller base limits the total impact.

India is the largest opportunity by volume: it contributes 86,506 starts and converts at 4.38%, below the 4.57% overall rate. The UAE is materially stronger, especially at payment completion. Singapore is the weakest listed geography, with a 3.69% overall conversion and 58.65% pay-to-purchase drop, but its base is much smaller. Geo differences do not explain the primary application-to-document loss, which remains around 86–88% across major markets.

Destination variation is narrower than the main funnel loss. Among high-volume destinations, Indonesia and Thailand underperform modestly, while Australia, Oman, and France are stronger. Korea has the lowest observed conversion and highest application-to-document drop in the displayed set, but its sample is relatively small.

Recommended priorities

  1. Fix the document-upload funnel first. Investigate why 86.76% of starts do not produce a document upload, with Android capture and threshold failures as the first diagnostic target.
  2. Prioritize Android remediation. Android has roughly 1.9 times the iOS document threshold-crossing rate and a lower overall conversion rate.
  3. Investigate payment completion on Android, web/Mac, Singapore, and India. These cohorts show elevated pay-to-purchase loss, with India offering the largest volume opportunity.
  4. Use destination-specific interventions selectively. Destination effects are real but comparatively small; avoid treating them as the primary cause until the document step is improved.
  5. Next analysis: cross-tab device × geography × destination and add citizenship, retry_count, payment method, and payment failure reason to distinguish product friction from eligibility or payment-mix effects.
