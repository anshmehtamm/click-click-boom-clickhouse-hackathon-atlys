Funnel investigation: the dominant loss is before and during document capture

Executive conclusion: The funnel has two material problems, but they are not equal in size. The largest is application_started → document_uploaded; the second is pay_now_clicked → purchase_completed. Android is the strongest diagnostic segment for the first problem.

Overall application-start to purchase conversion is 4.57% (7,054 / 154,413). Improving the document-upload bottleneck should be the first priority because it affects the largest population and precedes every downstream opportunity.

2. Priority issue: document capture is the dominant bottleneck

Only 20,446 of 154,413 started applications upload a document. Among uploaded documents, 88.8% do not cross the configured failed-attempt threshold, but this pass-rate metric describes users who already reached upload; it does not explain why nearly 87% of application starters never reach that stage. The investigation should therefore separate:

  • users who abandon before initiating capture;
  • users who initiate capture but never upload;
  • users who upload and fail or retry; and
  • users who pass capture but do not continue to payment.

The current tables can quantify the latter two groups, but they do not expose a complete pre-upload interaction sequence. Additional instrumentation around camera permission, capture initiation, upload errors, and abandonment is warranted.

Android is approximately 7.5 percentage points below iOS on document pass rate and 8.4 points below iOS on document-to-pay. Its failed-threshold rate is approximately 16.2%, versus 8.7% on iOS—directionally consistent with the known passport-scan model issue. The data does not establish whether the gap began after April, so the rollout-timing claim remains unproven.

Recommended action: prioritize Android capture diagnostics and compare model/version, camera pipeline, permissions, device family, and retry outcomes. Also investigate why 18% of Android rows have NULL OS, since the NULL-OS cohort has similarly weak document performance.

4. Downstream payment loss is real but lacks a single obvious culprit

Only 7,054 of 14,739 pay-now applications purchase, a 47.9% conversion. Payment method performance is tightly grouped: card 47.6%, UPI 48.6%, netbanking 45.9%, wallet 48.4%, and Apple Pay 48.1%. Coupon presence is also neutral: 47.6% with a coupon versus 47.9% without.

This suggests the payment problem is more likely to involve generic checkout friction, authentication/OTP, payment failures, or event coverage than one payment method or promotion. The existing issue about iOS WebKit OTP autofill is not supported by this data: iOS has the best major-OS pay-to-purchase result.

5. Other segment findings

  • Capture mode: camera, gallery, and QR have nearly identical document pass rates (88.7%, 88.7%, and 89.4%); no capture-mode-specific failure is visible.
  • Destination: MY is lowest among larger destination cohorts at 44.5% pay-to-purchase, but the spread is not large enough to explain the overall funnel collapse.
  • Citizenship: citizenship differences are modest and do not establish an MRZ-script effect. A script or OCR-confidence field is needed.
  • Acquisition: paid search and organic application-start-to-purchase conversion are almost identical at 4.65% and 4.55%.

6. Measurement caveat

Counts after document upload were matched by application_id and treated as order-agnostic. This is intentional: timestamps are not reliably ordered for document-upload → pay-now and pay-now → purchase, so windowFunnel or sequenceMatch would materially undercount the funnel. The data also has approximately 3% duplicate_id and 2% backfilled rows; deduplication and backfill monitoring should be included in production reporting.

7. Recommended priority order

  1. Instrument and fix the Android document-capture path, especially failed attempts, permissions, model behavior, and NULL OS attribution.
  2. Add pre-upload funnel events to locate the 86.8% application-start to upload loss.
  3. Instrument payment failure and authentication outcomes rather than relying only on pay-now and purchase events.
  4. Add MRZ script/OCR confidence and explicit error codes to test the non-Latin-passport hypothesis directly.
  5. Retain application_id-matched reporting for downstream conversion and avoid timestamp-sequence metrics past document upload.
